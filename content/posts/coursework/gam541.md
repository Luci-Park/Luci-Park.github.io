At DigiPen, a MSCS student is required to take three GAM classes, which includes making a game engine then creating a game on top of it. It's to teach the technology that goes into games and utilizing it. Not to mention working together as a group. 
For Spring 26, our first GAM class, the assignment was to build a 2D engine from scratch, run three game prototypes on it, and grow one of them into a game. We were given 13 weeks to make this work, alongside other work we had as master students.
Our team of five made a rhythm action platformer called Aural Mischief, and I served as tech lead and Design Sprint facilitator.
